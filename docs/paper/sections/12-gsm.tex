\section{Checking the conditions at build time}\label{sec:gsm}

gsm~\cite{gsm} is a Go library that runs the exact conditions of this paper on concrete systems. A
team declares its state as typed variables, its business rules as invariants with a repair, and its
events as raw transitions. Building the machine enumerates the finite state space and checks that
repair terminates (WFC) and that the two commutation conditions of \cref{thm:cc} hold. If they do, the
build returns a machine that applies an event with one table lookup; if not, it returns no machine and
reports a pair of events, a state, and the two orders that diverge. For the shelf of
\cref{ex:shelf}, written with a domain of 0 to 8 and clamping at both ends, the build refuses the
machine and reports a divergence on the empty shelf: ``add 3'' then ``remove 1'' gives 2, the other
order gives 3.

\paragraph{Checked twice.} The verdict of the Go code is not trusted on its own. A checker extracted
from the axiom-free Coq development re-certifies the machine's tables in process, and the build fails
closed if it does not. Relative to a trusted base, the extraction mechanism, the glue that calls the
generated code, and the translation of gsm's tables into its input, a bug in gsm's own verification
cannot hand back a machine that does not converge.

\paragraph{Scale.} The state space of a realistic system is a product of variable domains and quickly
exceeds what can be enumerated. gsm checks it in pieces, each justified by a theorem of the
development: by footprint component, the groups of variables that events and rules touch together,
which is exact for the whole machine when the rules are combinators; by one item of a keyed
collection, which stands for any number of items; by representative values for integer variables that
are only compared and copied; and, for networks of registries, by two local checks per link (called C1 and C2 in gsm and the earlier papers; they are federation
conditions, not the layer~C conditions of \cref{sec:layer-c})
on acyclic networks, with opt-in modes for monotone cycles and for cycles with a named coordination
plan. The cost is then governed by the largest piece, not by the product of all domains.

\paragraph{Changes in flight.} A rules or schema change made while events are in flight is classified
before deployment as safe at any time, safe behind a barrier, unsafe with a replayable witness, or
unknown when a search bound is hit, under the delivery model the deployment actually has.

\paragraph{What is not exact.} gsm's checks are sufficient forms of the reachable-state conditions:
they range over the whole enumerated state space, not only the states a system can reach, so a machine
can be refused although it would converge from its actual start. Domains must be finite, and a single
tightly coupled group of variables that is too large to enumerate is out of reach. Every guarantee in
a build report cites the theorem behind it, and a test keeps those citations equal to the gated names.
