\section{Limitations and what is not claimed}\label{sec:limits}

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \emph{No new mathematics in the tools.} Descent is compatibility with a kernel; Newman's lemma,
    trace theory, constraint-satisfaction algebra and fair cycles are classical and credited where they
    are used. The contribution is the exact conditions, the map, the mechanization and the framework's
    tests, not new technique.
  \item \emph{The three layers are not one principle.} Descent unifies the single-event conditions;
    across scales the conditions form a hierarchy with proved separations (\cref{prop:separations}).
    Layer~B is not a descent condition.
  \item \emph{Recoveries compress; they do not confirm.} The framework was written after the results it
    recovers. Outside theorems and two preregistered predictions are the evidence of reach, and two
    predictions are a small sample.
  \item \emph{The framework does not derive answers by theorem alone.} P2 was a bet on which side of a
    proved distinction a system falls; P3 was a model computation checked against running software.
  \item \emph{Layer~C is exact on finite state spaces.} The settlement recoveries are of finite
    fragments, and the strong form of C1 is classical in general.
  \item \emph{A unique solution does not give convergence} (\cref{prop:flip2}), and arc consistency
    does not decide existence without the chain and monotonicity hypotheses.
  \item \emph{gsm's static checks are sufficient, not exact.}
  \item \emph{Scope.} ``Exact'' is relative to the model each result states. Byzantine participants,
    nondeterministic repair, probabilistic schedules, infinite networks and real-time semantics are
    excluded by design.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

Repair can replace raw commutativity exactly when the result after repair does not depend on the
order. At one event this requires state descent; together with history descent over finite histories,
and terminating repair, it is exact for a single registry; over infinite runs on finite state spaces,
canonical recurrence is exact. The conditions form a proved hierarchy, and counterexamples fix the
qualifiers they name. A three-layer framework with proved interfaces organizes them across a map of
regimes, recovers the central theorem without circularity, transports to classical theorems from
outside its development with its own files frozen, and has made two correct preregistered
predictions, one of them about a widely used database.

The open problems that matter most are composition on cycles, where most of the map's open questions
sit; long-run behavior beyond finite state spaces; a way to combine concurrent states or steps in the
execution layer, which the CRDT and self-stabilization tests pointed to; and an exact structural condition that decides, for a
class of systems, when a unique consistent state is also reached, which would let the framework compute
answers where it can now only pose the question.
