\title{Normalization confluence: an exact, mechanized theory of\\when repair can replace commutativity}
\author{Dayna Blackwell}
\date{Draft}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Replicas that apply the same events in different orders, and repair their business invariants after
each event, need not end in the same state. We give an exact theory of when they do: raw states may
differ, but the result after repair must agree. We state a condition at each of three
scales: state descent, necessary at a single event; history descent over finite histories, which with
state descent and terminating repair is necessary and sufficient for a single registry; and canonical
recurrence, necessary and sufficient for infinite runs on finite state spaces. We prove the hierarchy
between them and show that CRDTs are the case where
repair does nothing: repair enlarges which operations can be used, while every convergent repaired
system is a CRDT on its repaired steps. A three-layer framework for executions, consistent states and
long-run behavior, with proved interfaces, organizes these conditions across a map of regimes that has,
in every regime, an exact condition, a hardness result or a listed open problem. We test the framework
where it could fail: with its files frozen it recovers Dijkstra's self-stabilization and invariant
confluence, and two predictions committed before their tests were confirmed, one about stable matching
markets and one about replication in CouchDB and PouchDB. All 4{,}766 gated results are mechanized in Coq with
no axioms.
\end{abstract}
