\begin{abstract}
\setlength{\parskip}{0.6\baselineskip plus 2pt minus 1pt}
Normalization confluence gives coordination-free convergence within a single registry, and its
federated extension gives convergence across a network of registries connected by morphisms, under
two decidable regimes: acyclic networks, and monotone repair on cycles~\cite{blackwell2026nc,
blackwell2026federated}. Those regimes are stated as separate sufficient conditions. This paper
gives the structure underneath them and, with it, an answer to a sharper question: when must a
federation coordinate at all, and how little coordination suffices?

We show that a federation's convergent states are a limit and its federated normalizer is the
retraction onto that limit, so compositionality (a verified sub-federation collapses to an effective
registry) is a corollary of one structural fact rather than a per-topology argument. We then show
that convergence certificates form a sheaf whose gluing condition is exactly the source-determinacy
and validity-preservation hypotheses the federation already certifies, so the obstruction to a global
convergent state is cohomological: $\Hz$ is the convergent states and $\Ho$ is the holonomy of the
morphism cycles. The two convergence regimes are then one picture: an acyclic network has no cycles
and $\Ho$ vanishes, while a monotone cycle collapses the obstruction to a least fixed point. The
obstruction is computable as a loop composite over the shared subspace, implemented in the reference
engine as a cycle diagnostic that names an obstructing cycle and exhibits its orbit. Finally we show
that when the obstruction is nonzero, coordinating a cycle basis of the morphism network suffices to
converge, localizing the global coordination verdict of CALM and invariant confluence into a
mixed-consistency partition. The exact minimum coordination is the group feedback edge set number of
the labeled nerve: NP-hard even in the abelian case, but fixed-parameter tractable in the size of the
coordinated core and polynomial on planar or edge-disjoint nerves. The structural core (the limit,
the retraction, and compositionality) is mechanized axiom-free in Coq/Rocq.
\end{abstract}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Federated Convergence, Normalization Confluence, Sheaf
Cohomology, Coordination Avoidance, CALM, Category Theory, Limits, Holonomy, Minimal Coordination,
Mechanized Proof

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Normalization confluence certifies that a single registry converges: under well-founded compensation
and compensation commutativity, every processor that consumes the same events reaches the same valid
state regardless of order~\cite{blackwell2026nc}. Its federated extension certifies that a network of
such registries, connected by directed morphisms that carry one registry's authority over another's
shared state, converges as a whole, in two regimes: any acyclic network whose morphisms preserve
validity, and any network (cyclic included) whose repair is monotone~\cite{blackwell2026federated}.
Both regimes are established by finite enumeration and are what the reference engine checks at build
time.

Those two regimes are presented as independent sufficient conditions, proved by separate arguments.
That leaves a structural question unanswered and a practical one unasked. The structural question:
is there one reason both regimes work, rather than two? The practical question: when a federation
falls into neither regime, the engine rejects it, but rejection is a blunt verdict. A designer wants
to know not merely that the network cannot converge coordination-free, but \emph{where} the conflict
is and \emph{how little} coordination would repair it. This paper answers both, and the answers turn
out to be the same object viewed twice.

\paragraph{The local-to-global view.}
A federation poses a local-to-global problem. Each registry is convergent on its own; each morphism,
in isolation, is a clean rule for propagating authority. The question is whether, once every morphism
constraint holds at once, there is a single global assignment the whole network agrees on, and
whether it is reached regardless of event order. This is the shape of a limit, and of a sheaf gluing
problem, and the obstruction to it is the shape of a cohomology class. Making that precise is what
unifies the regimes: an acyclic network has no cycles to obstruct gluing, and a monotone cycle has an
order that forces gluing even when a cycle is present. The same $\Ho$ that measures the obstruction
also, when it is nonzero, pinpoints the cycles responsible, which is exactly the diagnostic and the
minimal-coordination answers the practical question wants.

\paragraph{Scope, stated up front.}
The cleanest form of the cohomological classification holds in the \emph{invertible} fragment, where
each morphism transports its shared value by a bijection (a lossless relabeling: a copy, a
permutation, a bijective unit or schema map). There the transitions are group elements and $\Ho$ is
genuine \v{C}ech cohomology; a cycle basis is a sufficient coordinated core, though computing the
minimum one is NP-hard even here (\Cref{sec:minimal}). When morphisms collapse information (a clamp, a reset, a most-restrictive-wins merge,
the shape most compensation takes), the transitions are monoid elements rather than group elements,
classical $\Ho$ does not apply, and the correct statement is the operational one: a global convergent
state exists iff the loop composite of each cycle has a reachable fixed point. We therefore lead with
the general, operational results (the limit, the retraction, compositionality, and the loop-composite
diagnostic), which hold for arbitrary morphisms and are what the engine implements, and present the
cohomology as the sharp closed form on the invertible fragment. We state which real federations
inhabit that fragment (Section~\ref{sec:minimal}) rather than leaving it as ``the case where the math
is clean.''

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Federation as a limit, and compositionality for free
  (Sections~\ref{sec:limit}--\ref{sec:compose}).} A registry's valid set is the equalizer of its
  idempotent normalizer, a federation's convergent set is the equalizer of its shared-projection and
  resolver maps, and the federated normalizer is the idempotent retraction onto that limit. Because
  the normal forms are a limit, a convergent sub-federation collapses to an effective registry and
  the combined normalizer factors through it; compositionality is a corollary of one structural fact,
  not a per-topology theorem.
\item \textbf{Convergence certificates form a sheaf, and $R1/R2$ is its gluing axiom
  (Section~\ref{sec:sheaf}).} Local convergence certificates glue to a global one exactly when the
  subsystems agree as normalizers on their overlap. The two regimes that force agreement,
  single-writer authority and monotone overlap, are the federation's source-determinacy ($R1$) and
  validity-preservation ($R2$) hypotheses. The existing federation design is validated as the sheaf
  gluing check, not extended by a new mechanism.
\item \textbf{The obstruction is cohomological, and it unifies the two regimes
  (Section~\ref{sec:completion}).} $\Hz$ is the convergent states; $\Ho$ (in the invertible fragment)
  is the holonomy of the morphism cycles, generated by a cycle basis. $\Ho$ vanishes in exactly two
  ways, and they are the two convergence regimes: an acyclic network (no cycles) and a monotone cycle
  (a least fixed point). The dichotomy the base work states as two conditions is one invariant.
\item \textbf{A computable obstruction diagnostic (Section~\ref{sec:diagnostic}).} For a general
  (possibly non-invertible) cycle, a global convergent state exists iff the cycle's loop composite has
  a reachable fixed point, and the witness for its absence is the loop composite itself, computable
  over the shared subspace rather than the product. This is implemented in the reference engine as a
  cycle diagnostic that names the offending cycle and reports whether its repair settles or orbits.
\item \textbf{Minimal coordination (Section~\ref{sec:minimal}).} When the obstruction is nonzero,
  coordinating a cycle basis of the morphism network trivializes it, yielding a convergent
  implementation that is strongly consistent only on those loops and coordination-free everywhere
  else. This refines the global coordination verdict of
  CALM~\cite{hellerstein2010declarative,ameloot2013relational} and invariant
  confluence~\cite{bailis2014coordination} into a localized, mixed-consistency partition, and it
  upgrades the engine's blunt rejection of a nonconvergent cycle into an actionable ``coordinate
  these loops.'' We identify the exact minimum with the group feedback edge set number of the
  labeled nerve, resolving its complexity: NP-hard even in the abelian case (it contains edge
  bipartization), fixed-parameter tractable in the size of the coordinated core, and polynomial on
  planar or edge-disjoint nerves; and we show the minimum is never below the abelian count and can be
  strictly above it, so abelianized sizing is unsound.
\item \textbf{Mechanization.} The structural core (Contributions~1 and~2: the limit, the retraction
  for arbitrary acyclic federations, and compositionality) is mechanized axiom-free in Coq/Rocq,
  extending the machine-checked development of the base results.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Sheaf cohomology for distributed systems: the same tool, a different object.}
The use of sheaf cohomology to measure a local-to-global obstruction is not new, and we do not claim
it. Cellular-sheaf cohomology characterizes solvability of distributed decision \emph{tasks}, where
terminating solutions are the global sections and the cohomology encodes the obstruction to a
solution~\cite{felber2025sheaftasks}, in the combinatorial-topology lineage of distributed
computing~\cite{herlihy2013topology}; the same presheaf-and-obstruction template measures quantum
contextuality~\cite{abramsky2011sheaf}. Our object is different. Those works concern which one-shot
decision task a set of processes can solve under a failure or adversary model. We concern the
convergence of a \emph{federated normalization}: a network of registries, each with invariants and
compensation, whose morphisms must agree on shared state so that all processors reach the same valid
global normal form regardless of event order. Our presheaf is over the overlaps of a normalizer
structure, its sections are convergence certificates, $\Hz$ is the convergent states, and $\Ho$ is
the holonomy of the morphism cycles. To our knowledge no prior sheaf-cohomology work targets the
eventual-consistency or replicated-convergence obstruction, and none yields a coordination-minimality
result.

\paragraph{Coordination avoidance: a global verdict we localize.}
The CALM theorem states that a problem has a coordination-free, consistent implementation iff it is
monotone~\cite{hellerstein2010declarative, ameloot2013relational}, and invariant
confluence~\cite{bailis2014coordination} gives a related global avoidability criterion; recent work
sharpens the monotonicity boundary at the semantic level~\cite{completecalm2026,
coordfree2025}. These answer \emph{whether} coordination can be avoided, as a global property of the
whole problem. Our minimal-coordination result answers \emph{where} and \emph{how little}: it locates
the irreducible coordination in a cycle basis of the morphism network and shows the rest can remain
coordination-free, a localized mixed-consistency partition rather than a single yes-or-no. In the
information-preserving fragment the core is provably minimal. We are not aware of prior work that
localizes the coordination requirement to a cycle basis or bounds it by a Betti number.

\paragraph{Convergent replicated data and its algebra.}
Conflict-free replicated data types achieve convergence by restricting to operations that never need
repair; a state-based CRDT is the join-semilattice special case~\cite{shapiro2011crdt}, and the
monotone regime here coincides with it (this correspondence is machine-checked in the base
development). The well-founded-compensation regime is strictly broader, since it admits operations
that violate invariants and are repaired. Category-theoretic accounts of CRDT convergence exist at
the level of the merge algebra; they do not give a cohomological obstruction or a
coordination-minimality theorem. The lattice-theoretic and chaotic-iteration machinery behind the
monotone regime is classical~\cite{tarski1955, cousot1977}, as is the confluence core~\cite{newman1942}.

\paragraph{Invariant repair and mechanized convergence.}
The base development sits in the invariant-repair lineage: Indigo~\cite{balegas2015indigo} and
IPA~\cite{balegas2019ipa} also restore invariants over weakly consistent replicas, by reservation and
invariant-confluence-preserving analysis rather than by confluence of a compensating rewrite system.
Its method roots are Meseguer's rewriting logic and coherence~\cite{meseguer1992, duran2012coherence}
and self-stabilization~\cite{dijkstra1974}. On the mechanization side, Gomes et
al.~\cite{gomes2017verifying} machine-check strong eventual consistency for CRDTs in Isabelle/HOL; the
base convergence theorem over which we build the categorical structure is likewise machine-checked and
axiom-free, for the broader compensation regime. None of these give a cohomological obstruction or a
coordination-minimality theorem, which is the object of this paper.

\section{Background}
\label{sec:background}

We restate only what later sections use; full statements and proofs are in the base
papers~\cite{blackwell2026nc, blackwell2026federated}.

\paragraph{Single registry.} A registry governs a finite state space by a validity predicate and a
compensation (repair) map. Its normalizer $\rho_R$ iterates compensation to a valid state. Two
conditions make this well-behaved: \emph{well-founded compensation} (WFC), a compensation measure
that strictly decreases on every invalid state, so compensation terminates and $\rho_R$ is total; and
\emph{compensation commutativity} (CC), the local-confluence condition on event and repair steps.
Under WFC and CC, Newman's Lemma~\cite{newman1942} gives unique normal forms: every processor that
consumes the same events reaches the same valid state regardless of order. WFC also makes $\rho_R$
idempotent (a normal form is a fixed point) and its valid set non-empty (compensation reaches a valid
state from any state), the two facts \Cref{sec:limit} uses.

\paragraph{Federation.} Registries are connected by directed \emph{morphisms}. A morphism
$\phi : A \to B$ makes $A$ authoritative over a designated \emph{shared} component of $B$: given
$A$'s normal form, $\phi$ fixes $B$'s shared variables, leaving $B$'s local variables to $B$'s own
compensation. The core mechanism is the \emph{authority argument}: because the source has a unique
normal form, it deterministically fixes the target's shared component, eliminating the nondeterminism
a symmetric constraint would introduce. A single-source morphism must preserve validity under
overwrite ($M1$): overwriting a valid target's shared component with the morphism image of a valid
source keeps the target valid. A target with several sources carries a \emph{resolver} that merges
them, subject to source-determinacy ($R1$: it reads only the sources) and validity preservation
($R2$: the single-source $M1$ generalized). The base work proves federated convergence in two
regimes, each by finite enumeration: any acyclic network whose morphisms satisfy $M1/R1/R2$, and any
network (cycles included) whose repair is monotone (least fixed point by Knaster-Tarski~\cite{tarski1955},
reached by chaotic iteration~\cite{cousot1977}). Those two regimes are what this paper explains as one
structure.

\section{Normal Forms Are a Limit}
\label{sec:limit}

We work at the semantic level. A registry $R$ has a finite state space $D_R$ and a \emph{normalizer}
$\rho_R : D_R \to D_R$, the iterated-compensation map, which is total (termination, from well-founded
compensation) and idempotent ($\rho_R \circ \rho_R = \rho_R$: a normal form is a fixed point, from
confluence plus termination). Write $\Phi_R \defeq \mathrm{im}(\rho_R)$ for the valid set, the normal
forms.

Everything in this section is elementary in $\mathbf{Set}$, and the load-bearing facts are mechanized
axiom-free in Coq/Rocq (module \texttt{Categorical.v}); we state them and sketch the arguments,
naming the mechanized lemmas.

\subsection{A single registry is an equalizer}

\begin{lemma}[Lemma 0]
\label{lem:zero}
For an idempotent normalizer $\rho_R$, the valid set, the fixed-point set, and the equalizer of the
identity and $\rho_R$ coincide:
\[ \Phi_R = \mathrm{im}(\rho_R) = \mathrm{Fix}(\rho_R) = \mathrm{eq}(\mathrm{id}_{D_R}, \rho_R), \]
a limit in $\mathbf{Set}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Idempotence gives $\mathrm{im}(\rho_R) = \mathrm{Fix}(\rho_R)$: if $s = \rho_R(x)$ then
$\rho_R(s) = \rho_R(\rho_R(x)) = \rho_R(x) = s$, and conversely a fixed point is its own image. And
$\mathrm{Fix}(\rho_R) = \{s : \mathrm{id}(s) = \rho_R(s)\}$ is by definition the equalizer of
$\mathrm{id}$ and $\rho_R$, a limit. Mechanized as \texttt{image\_iff\_fixed} and
\texttt{fixed\_is\_equalizer}.
\end{proof}

Read as a retraction onto $\Phi_R$ (it fixes $\Phi_R$ and lands in it), $\rho_R$ is an idempotent
monad on $\mathbf{Set}$ whose algebras are the normal forms, and a closure operator on the ordered
domains of the cyclic regime. We need only the equalizer.

\subsection{A federation is a finite limit}

Fix a federation: registries $R_1, \dots, R_n$, and for each target $B$ with source set
$\mathrm{src}(B)$, shared variables $\mathrm{sh}(B)$ and a resolver $\Res_B$ merging its sources'
normal forms into $B$'s shared component (for a single source this is the morphism's map). The base
papers certify by finite enumeration that each $\Res_B$ reads only its sources ($R1$) and preserves
target validity ($R2$; for a single source, $M1$). Let $g$ send a tuple of component normal forms to
the tuple of the targets' current shared components, and $h$ send it to the tuple of resolver values.
The \emph{consistent set} is
\[ L_F \defeq \Big\{ (s_1, \dots, s_n) \in \textstyle\prod_i \Phi_{R_i} :
   \text{for every target } B,\ s_B \restriction \mathrm{sh}(B)
   = \Res_B\big((s_{A})_{A \in \mathrm{src}(B)}\big) \Big\}. \]

\begin{proposition}[Proposition 1]
\label{prop:one}
$L_F = \mathrm{eq}(g, h)$. Since each $\Phi_{R_i}$ is a limit (\Cref{lem:zero}) and $L_F$ is an
equalizer of a product of these, $L_F$ is a finite limit in $\mathbf{Set}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A tuple lies in $L_F$ iff every target's shared component equals its resolver value, that is, iff $g$
and $h$ agree on it componentwise, that is, iff $g = h$ there. Componentwise agreement over the finite
target set is equality of the two tuple-valued maps, so $L_F$ is their equalizer. Mechanized as
\texttt{consistent\_iff\_equalizer}, with the product over targets modeled as a list so the
characterization needs no functional extensionality.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The federated normalizer is the retraction onto the limit}

Fix a topological order (sources before targets). The federated normalizer $\rho_F$ processes
registries in that order: for each, overwrite its shared component with the resolver applied to the
already-finalized sources, then apply the local normalizer. We model this as a left-to-right fold
over the ordered components, writing $\mathrm{step}$ for the per-position operation (read finalized
prefix, overwrite shared, normalize). The one hypothesis it needs is that $\mathrm{step}$ is
idempotent given a fixed finalized prefix, which is exactly $M1/R2$ (the local normalizer fixes the
overwritten shared component) together with local idempotence.

\begin{theorem}[Theorem 1: federation retraction, acyclic]
\label{thm:one}
$\rho_F$ is an idempotent retraction onto $L_F$: $\mathrm{im}(\rho_F) = \mathrm{Fix}(\rho_F) = L_F$.
Equivalently, the federated normal forms are exactly the finite limit of \Cref{prop:one} and $\rho_F$
is the retraction onto it. Moreover $\rho_F$ is independent of the chosen topological order.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Two facts suffice and yield the retraction by a general argument: any operator that is sound and
complete for a set $L$ is the idempotent retraction onto $L$, with image and fixed set $L$
(mechanized as \texttt{RetractionOntoConsistent}). \emph{Soundness} (Lemma A): $\rho_F(s) \in L_F$,
because each finalized position is $\mathrm{step}$ of its finalized prefix, which
$\mathrm{step}$-idempotence leaves fixed (\texttt{rhoFold\_sound}). \emph{Completeness} (Lemma B): a
consistent state is already fixed, since each position's step is a no-op on it; this uses only the
definition of $L_F$, not $M1/R2$ (\texttt{rhoFold\_complete}). Hence $\rho_F$ is the idempotent
retraction onto $L_F$ (\texttt{rhoFold\_retraction}, \texttt{rhoFold\_image\_iff\_consistent}), for
arbitrary acyclic federations, not a fixed small instance.

Order-independence reduces to the commutation of two incomparable registries: their reads and writes
are disjoint, so processing them in either order gives the same result (\texttt{updates\_commute}).
Lifting this to all topological orders uses the classical fact that the linear extensions of a finite
poset are connected by adjacent transpositions of incomparable elements, which we take as given
rather than mechanize.
\end{proof}

\section{Compositionality for Free}
\label{sec:compose}

Because the federated normal forms are a limit and $\rho_F$ is the retraction onto it, a convergent
sub-federation behaves as a single effective registry inside a larger one. Split a federation along a
topological cut into an upstream block $J$ (closed under sources: every source of a $J$-registry is
itself in $J$) and the rest $K$. The \emph{flat} normalizer runs $\rho_F$ over all of $J \cup K$ in
one order; the \emph{staged} normalizer runs the sub-federation's normalizer $\rho_J$ to completion,
then runs $K$ over the finalized $J$-block.

\begin{theorem}[Theorem 2: compositionality]
\label{thm:two}
The flat and staged normalizers agree: normalizing $J \cup K$ in one pass equals normalizing $J$,
then normalizing $K$ over the finalized result. Consequently a verified sub-federation collapses to
an effective registry (its valid set $L_J$), and the combined normalizer factors through it.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
At the operator level this is the fold-append law: folding $J$ and then continuing with $K$ from the
accumulated result equals folding the concatenation, because the accumulator after $J$ is exactly
$\rho_J$'s output and each $K$-step reads only its own finalized sources. Mechanized as
\texttt{rhoFold\_compositional}, via \texttt{rhoF\_from\_app}, axiom-free.
\end{proof}

This is what lets verification scale: a subsystem is checked once, in isolation, and reused as a
black box, with only the seam (the morphisms crossing the cut into $K$) re-checked. The
assume-guarantee refinement makes the black box parametric. A sub-federation may declare \emph{input
ports}: shared variables that no internal morphism writes, which an outer morphism may drive once the
subsystem is embedded. A seam morphism into a port is verified against $M1/R2$ while the subsystem's
interior is trusted from its certificate, and a write to any non-port (sealed) variable is rejected.
We treat the port refinement at the paper level here; the operator-level collapse above is the
mechanized core.

\section{Convergence Certificates Form a Sheaf}
\label{sec:sheaf}

The limit of \Cref{sec:limit} is a global object assembled from local ones, the shape of a sheaf.
Making that precise both explains why the federation's hypotheses are the right ones and sets up the
obstruction of \Cref{sec:completion}.

\paragraph{The presheaf.} Over a system's variables, take a region to be a variable subset $U$, the
subsystem on $U$ to be the rules whose footprint lies in $U$, and the section $F(U)$ to be that
subsystem's normalizer $\rho_U$ together with its convergence verdict. Restriction $F(U) \to F(U')$
for $U' \subseteq U$ restricts the normalizer. The sheaf question is whether local certificates that
agree on overlaps glue to a global one, checkable from the overlap alone.

\paragraph{Gluing is not naive.} Agreement on which shared values are \emph{valid} is not enough.

\begin{example}
\label{ex:glue}
Let $s \in \{0,1,2\}$. Subsystem $A$ has invariant $s \neq 1$ with repair $s := 0$; subsystem $B$ has
invariant $s \neq 1$ with repair $s := 2$. Each is confluent, and both have valid set $\{0, 2\}$, so
they agree on which values are valid. But in the union, from $s = 1$ the result is $0$ (running $A$
first) or $2$ (running $B$ first): order-dependent, non-confluent.
\end{example}

\paragraph{The precise gluing condition.} The obstruction in \Cref{ex:glue} is that $A$ and $B$
disagree as \emph{normalizers} on the shared state ($A : 1 \mapsto 0$, $B : 1 \mapsto 2$). So the
correct sense of ``agree on the overlap'' is that the two certificates restrict to the \emph{same
normal-form map} on the shared variables. Then the shared variable has one unambiguous normal form,
the union is confluent, and the sections glue: the global normalizer runs each private part plus the
agreed map on the overlap. This is checkable on the overlap alone, never on the joint product.
Convergence certificates therefore form a sheaf on the site whose overlaps satisfy this agreement.

\paragraph{Two regimes force agreement, and they are the federation's.} The overlap agreement holds
in two ways:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \emph{Single-writer overlap}: only one subsystem writes the shared variable, the other reads
  it. The writer's normalizer is the only one on the overlap, so agreement is automatic. This is the
  authority / $M1$ discipline.
\item \emph{Monotone overlap}: both write, but monotonically. The combined repair is a monotone
  operator with a unique least fixed point (Knaster-Tarski~\cite{tarski1955}), so the normalizers
  agree on the glued result. This is the monotone-cycles regime.
\end{itemize}

\begin{proposition}[$R1 + R2$ is the gluing axiom]
\label{prop:gluing}
In a federation, distinct components do not literally share a writable variable; a shared writable
variable is a multi-source target whose resolver merges its sources. The resolver hypotheses are
exactly the sheaf gluing condition: source-determinacy ($R1$) makes the merged normal form
single-valued (confluence on the overlap), and validity-preservation ($R2$) makes it land in the
agreed valid set. Hence the federation's resolver verification \emph{is} the sheaf gluing check.
\end{proposition}

The existing federation design is thus validated by the sheaf structure rather than extended by a new
primitive: the machinery that composes overlapping subsystems soundly is already present as $R1/R2$.
The gluing counterexample (\Cref{ex:glue}) is mechanized axiom-free (module \texttt{Cohomology.v});
\Cref{prop:gluing} and the sheaf assembly over the full cover are at the paper level.

\section{The Cohomological Completion}
\label{sec:completion}

For two subsystems the gluing condition of \Cref{sec:sheaf} is a single equality check, so nothing
subtle survives. The interesting case is a cycle of three or more subsystems whose pairwise overlaps
each agree yet admit no global section: the classic local-consistency, global-impossibility
phenomenon, measured by the first \v{C}ech cohomology of the presheaf over the nerve of the overlap
cover.

\paragraph{The loop composite.} Take a triangle in which $A$ and $B$ share $x$, $B$ and $C$ share
$y$, $C$ and $A$ share $z$, the cycle's morphisms fixing each next shared component from the previous
normal form. A global section is a shared assignment fixed all the way around, so the shared value
must be a fixed point of the loop composite $g = m_{CA} \circ m_{BC} \circ m_{AB}$ on the shared
subspace.

\begin{theorem}[Obstruction, general case]
\label{thm:obstruction}
A global convergent section around a cycle exists iff the cycle's loop composite $g$ has a reachable
fixed point, and the witness for its absence is $g$ itself, computable over the shared subspace
rather than the product.
\end{theorem}

The minimal obstruction has shared values in $\{0,1\}$, two edges the identity and one the negation,
so $g$ is the flip, which has no fixed point (it orbits $0 \mapsto 1 \mapsto 0$): no consistent global
assignment exists, and the witness points at the negation edge.

\paragraph{The completion, invertible fragment.} Assemble the sheaf on the nerve $N$ of the cover: a
vertex per subsystem, an edge per non-empty overlap, a triangle per triple overlap. Write the shared
fiber on an overlap as a finite set $S$; in the \emph{invertible} fragment (each morphism transports
its shared value by a bijection) the identification across edge $(i,j)$ is a bijection $g_{ij}$ in a
group $G \leq \mathrm{Sym}(S)$, with the cocycle condition $g_{ik} = g_{jk} \cdot g_{ij}$ on
triangles.

$\Hz$ is the convergent states: a global section assigns each subsystem $i$ a shared value $s_i$ with
$g_{ij} \cdot s_i = s_j$ on every overlap, which is exactly a consistent global normal form, so
$\Hz(N, F)$ is the consistent set $L_F$ of \Cref{prop:one}, and ``converges compositionally'' is
``$\Hz$ is non-empty.'' $\Ho$ is the obstruction: the $0$-cochains are gauges ($h_i \in G$ per
vertex, a relabeling of subsystem $i$'s shared normal form), the coboundary is
$(dh)_{ij} = h_j \cdot h_i^{-1}$, the $1$-cocycles are the transition families satisfying the triangle
condition, and $\Ho$ is the $1$-cocycles modulo gauge.

\begin{theorem}[Completion, invertible fragment]
\label{thm:completion}
When the shared fiber is a $G$-torsor, a global section exists iff the transition cocycle
$\{g_{ij}\}$ is trivial in $\Ho$ (a coboundary).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a base $c \in S$; freeness and transitivity make $g \mapsto g \cdot c$ a bijection $G \to S$, so
each $s_i = h_i \cdot c$ for a unique $h_i \in G$. If $g_{ij} = h_j \cdot h_i^{-1}$, set
$s_i = h_i \cdot c$; then $g_{ij} \cdot s_i = h_j \cdot h_i^{-1} \cdot h_i \cdot c = h_j \cdot c = s_j$,
a global section. Conversely a section $\{s_i\}$ with $g_{ij} \cdot s_i = s_j$ gives
$g_{ij} \cdot h_i = h_j$, so $g_{ij} = h_j \cdot h_i^{-1} = (dh)_{ij}$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{$\Ho$ is holonomy, and its generators are a cycle basis.} Fix a spanning tree of $N$ and
gauge the tree transitions to the identity; the cocycle is then determined by its values on the
non-tree edges, one per independent cycle, and each value is the holonomy around that cycle, the loop
composite $g$ of \Cref{thm:obstruction}. So $\Ho$, as a pointed set, is the holonomies of a cycle
basis modulo simultaneous conjugation, its rank is the first Betti number of the nerve, and it is
trivial iff every loop composite is the identity. The cycle basis is the minimal obstruction basis:
fixing the holonomy on each generator makes $\Hz$ non-empty (\Cref{sec:minimal}).

\paragraph{The two regimes are one picture.} The obstruction vanishes in exactly two ways, and they
are the two convergence regimes of the base work:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \emph{Trivial holonomy.} An acyclic nerve has no independent cycles (first Betti number $0$,
  $\Ho$ trivial); more generally trivial loop composites give a coboundary. This is the acyclic /
  tree route.
\item \emph{Monotone fibers.} When $S$ carries a lattice order and the transitions are monotone (not
  invertible, so outside the torsor picture), Knaster-Tarski gives a least fixed point of the loop
  composite, so $\Hz$ is non-empty regardless of holonomy. This is the monotone route.
\end{itemize}
The first kills the cohomological obstruction; the second sidesteps it via order. The dichotomy the
base papers state as two separate sufficient conditions is thus one invariant, and its non-vanishing
is exactly what the engine rejects.

\paragraph{Scope.} The clean cohomological ``iff'' of \Cref{thm:completion} is the invertible /
torsor fragment. When transitions are non-invertible (a repair that collapses values) the fiber is
not a torsor and the transitions are monoid elements, so classical $\Ho$ does not apply; there the
obstruction is $\Hz$ directly, via the reachable-fixed-point condition of \Cref{thm:obstruction}, a
dynamical rather than cohomological statement. The single-cycle essence of \Cref{thm:completion} (a
section exists iff the holonomy is trivial, in the invertible fragment), together with the gluing
counterexample and the negation obstruction, is mechanized axiom-free (\texttt{Cohomology.v}); the
assembly of the per-cycle holonomies into the nerve's $\Ho$ as a quotient, with cycle-basis
generators and Betti-number rank, is at the paper level, as is the cohomology-proper classification
(computing the $\Ho$ class itself and its non-abelian analogue). The operational fixed-point test is
implemented (\Cref{sec:diagnostic}).

%========================================
\section{The Obstruction Diagnostic}
\label{sec:diagnostic}

The obstruction of \Cref{thm:obstruction} is not only a classification but a computation. The witness
for a cycle's failure to converge is its loop composite $g$, and $g$ lives on the shared subspace, so
it is computed by composing the cycle's morphisms over that subspace, bounded by it rather than by
the product of the components. This is what makes the diagnostic tractable: a cycle through
registries with large local state spaces is analyzed on the small shared fiber alone.

\paragraph{Implementation.} The reference engine implements this as \texttt{Federation.DiagnoseCycle}.
It finds a directed cycle in the morphism network, iterates the cycle's repair from a representative
seed on the finite shared space (which must either reach a fixed point or repeat), and reports which,
returning the cycle, whether it converged, and, when it did not, the repeating configuration as an
orbit witness.

\begin{example}[Settle versus orbit]
\label{ex:diag}
Two registries with a shared value in $\{0,1\}$. If $A$ copies its value to $B$ and $B$ copies back
(both edges the identity), the loop composite is the identity: every value is a fixed point, and the
diagnostic reports convergence. If instead $B$ copies back the negation, the loop composite is the
flip, which orbits $0 \mapsto 1 \mapsto 0$; the diagnostic reports non-convergence and returns that
orbit as the witness, pointing at the negation edge.
\end{example}

\paragraph{Reading the verdict.} A non-convergent verdict is definitive: an orbit exists, so the
cycle cannot converge, and the orbit is a proof. A convergent verdict from a single seed is weaker:
it establishes that this seed settles and names the cycle for reference, but does not by itself prove
whole-network convergence, since another local state might still oscillate. The diagnostic is
therefore a sound refuter and a cycle namer, not a whole-network decision procedure; deciding
convergence outright is the exhaustive check the base engine already performs, and the diagnostic
explains a rejection rather than replacing that check.

\paragraph{What it delivers.} For federating $N$ independently-governed policies, the diagnostic
answers two questions the bare convergence verdict does not: can these compose, and, when they
cannot, where is the conflict. The answer is legible (it names the offending cycle and exhibits
either its orbit or its missing fixed point) and cheap (it composes the cycle's morphisms and tests
for a reachable fixed point over the shared subspace). Combined with \Cref{prop:minimal}, it is also
constructive: the cycles it flags are exactly the ones any coordinated core must cover.

\section{Minimal Coordination}
\label{sec:minimal}

Normalization confluence is a strong-eventual-consistency guarantee: replicas that consume the same
events reach the same valid state regardless of order. So the convergence question is a coordination
question. A coordination-free, strongly-eventually-consistent implementation exists exactly when a
consistent global normal form exists, that is, when $\Hz$ is non-empty, and the obstruction to it is
the $\Ho$ class of \Cref{sec:completion}. This is the CALM boundary (monotone implies
coordination-free) and invariant confluence, now not merely decided but \emph{classified}: the
completion says precisely which configurations obstruct, so it turns coordination into a graph
problem on the labeled nerve.

\begin{proposition}[Coordinating a cycle basis suffices]
\label{prop:minimal}
In the invertible fragment, a federation admits a coordination-free, strongly-eventually-consistent
convergent implementation iff $\Ho = 0$. When $\Ho \neq 0$, imposing a coordination boundary (a total
order, or a single writer) on the shared variable along a cycle basis trivializes the obstruction,
yielding a convergent implementation that is strongly consistent only on those loops and
coordination-free everywhere else.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Coordinating a loop forces its shared variable to a single agreed value, the single-writer overlap of
\Cref{sec:sheaf}, so that loop's transition becomes a coboundary and its holonomy generator dies.
Coordinating a cycle basis kills every generator, leaving the trivial class, so $\Hz$ becomes
non-empty by \Cref{thm:completion}; off the coordinated loops the transitions are unchanged and stay
coordination-free.
\end{proof}

So a federation decomposes into a coordination-free majority (eventual consistency) and a coordinated
core (strong consistency). For a distributed deployment this reads: put consensus only on the
obstructing cycles, run everything else coordination-free. It refines the global coordination verdict
of CALM~\cite{hellerstein2010declarative, ameloot2013relational} and invariant
confluence~\cite{bailis2014coordination} from a single yes-or-no into a localized, mixed-consistency
partition.

\paragraph{The minimum coordination, and its complexity.} A cycle basis suffices but is not minimal,
since one coordinated edge can break several obstructing cycles at once. The exact minimum is the
\emph{group feedback edge set} number of the labeled nerve: delete the fewest shared variables so
every residual cycle is balanced. This resolves its complexity. It is NP-hard even in the abelian
case, since for $G = \mathbb{Z}/2$ it is edge bipartization, the complement of Max Cut. Under the
Unique Games Conjecture it has no constant-factor approximation ($O(\sqrt{\log n})$, the Min-Uncut
bound, is the best known). But it is fixed-parameter tractable in the size of the coordinated core,
which is the operative regime since one wants that core small, and polynomial on planar nerves
(planar Max Cut) and on edge-disjoint obstructions (one hit per cycle, any $G$). A correct,
polynomial, size-$\le b$ coordination is always available: coordinate the orbiting fundamental cycles
of any spanning tree.

\paragraph{Non-commutativity raises the floor; it never lowers it.} The minimum is never below what
the abelianized holonomy suggests, and can be strictly above. A section after coordinating $F$ needs
$\pi_1(N \setminus F) \subseteq \ker \rho \subseteq \ker(\mathrm{ab} \circ \rho)$, so every feasible
$F$ is feasible for the abelianized problem and $\min_G \ge \min_{G^{\mathrm{ab}}}$. The inequality is
strict: on the theta graph with holonomies $(0\,1\,2)$ and $(0\,1)$ in $S_3$, the true minimum is
$2$, while the sign-abelianized count is $1$ because the $3$-cycle is even and invisible to the sign.
The consequence is a soundness warning: sizing coordination by any abelianized invariant
under-provisions, wrongly declaring coordination-free some cyclic federations that in fact need to
coordinate an even-permutation relabeling loop. The finite crux of this separation is machine-checked.

\paragraph{Which federations inhabit the cohomological fragment.} A morphism is invertible on the
shared fiber exactly when it transports the shared value by a bijection (a lossless relabeling: a
copy, a permutation, a bijective unit or schema map), and non-invertible when it collapses
information (a clamp, a reset, a most-restrictive-wins merge, which includes every multi-source
resolver). The clean cohomological classification is the invertible case; acyclic federations have
nothing to coordinate, monotone cycles converge by the least fixed point, and only invertible cycles
with non-trivial holonomy require the coordinated core. In the general non-invertible case the
decomposition still holds (a cycle basis suffices, by the fixed-point form of \Cref{thm:obstruction}),
with the same group-feedback-set minimum.

\paragraph{From rejection to repair.} The base engine rejects a non-monotone cyclic federation: it
cannot converge coordination-free, and the build fails. \Cref{prop:minimal} upgrades that blunt
verdict into an actionable one: rather than reject, accept the federation with a coordination
boundary on a cycle basis, which the diagnostic of \Cref{sec:diagnostic} identifies. This turns
``these policies do not compose'' into ``these policies compose if you coordinate exactly these
loops.''

\paragraph{Boundary.} This locates where coordination is needed for convergence, assuming the
delivery layer gives each replica the same event set (normalization confluence is the convergence
layer, not the transport). It is clean in the invertible fragment; the general non-invertible case
reduces to the loop-composite fixed-point condition, where coordinating a loop still means forcing
its shared variable to an agreed value but the minimal-basis count is not a cohomological rank.

%========================================
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Federated convergence has a structure, and naming it answers both a theoretical and a practical
question. Theoretically, a federation's convergent states are a limit and its normalizer is the
retraction onto that limit, so compositionality is a corollary rather than a separate theorem; the
convergence certificates form a sheaf whose gluing axiom is exactly the federation's already-certified
$R1/R2$; and the obstruction to a global convergent state is cohomological, with $\Hz$ the convergent
states and $\Ho$ the holonomy of the morphism cycles. The two convergence regimes of the base work,
acyclicity and monotone repair, are then one invariant: the first makes $\Ho$ vanish, the second
sidesteps it by order.

Practically, the same invariant is computable and actionable. Its witness is a cycle's loop composite
over the shared subspace, implemented as a diagnostic that names an obstructing cycle and exhibits its
orbit; and when the obstruction is non-zero, coordinating a cycle basis suffices to converge. This
localizes the global coordination verdict of CALM and invariant confluence into a mixed-consistency
partition, and it turns a rejection into a constructive ``coordinate exactly these loops.'' The exact
minimum coordination is the group feedback edge set number, NP-hard even in the abelian case but
fixed-parameter tractable in the core size and polynomial on planar or edge-disjoint nerves, and it
is never below the abelian count.

The structural core, the limit, the retraction for arbitrary acyclic federations, and
compositionality, is mechanized axiom-free. What is deliberately left at the paper level is scoped
plainly: the clean cohomological classification is the invertible (lossless-relabeling) fragment, the
general case reducing to the loop-composite fixed-point condition that the diagnostic already
computes; and full order-independence rests on the classical connectivity of a poset's linear
extensions. The open problems are the cohomology-proper $\Ho$ classification and its non-abelian
analogue, and mechanizing the cohomological layer to the standard the structural core already meets.
